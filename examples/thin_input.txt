The old lighthouse at Point Harrow was built in 1874 from granite quarried less than a mile away, and for nearly a century it guided coastal traffic past the shoals that had claimed at least a dozen ships before it was lit. The keeper's cottage, a squat two-room building on the leeward side, housed three generations of the same family until the light was automated in 1968. Today the tower is open to visitors on summer weekends. The climb is 112 steps up a spiral iron staircase, and on a clear day you can see the mainland ferry terminal and, beyond it, the faint line of the northern islands. The local historical society maintains the site with volunteer labor and a small grant from the county. Most visitors come for the view, but the society's docents are quick to point out the original Fresnel lens, still in place, which was shipped from Paris and took six months to arrive.
